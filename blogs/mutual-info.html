What is the Mutual Information $\small{I(X;Y=y)}$

Created Oct 9, 2026 ·  · 
Contents
  1. Introduction

Introduction

It is well known that the mutual information is defined between random variables $X$ and $Y$ as $I(X;Y) = \sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}=H(X)-H(X|Y)=H(Y)-H(Y|X)=D_{KL}\left(p(x,y) || p(x)p(y)\right).$ And also the pointwise mutual information is defined between two events (realizations) $x$ and $y$ as $I(x;y) = \log \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}.$

However, this is no single definition of $I(X;Y=y)$. There are two definitions have been discussed and used.

When I was an undergraduate student, I learned from the test book that $I(X;Y=y)$ is defined as $I(X;Y=y)$, but I was not fully convinced, since it is not equal to $H(X)-H(X|Y=y)$. I mean...how can they be not equal?! But at that time I could not find any materials that explicitly explain this.

Two years later, I learned from Emre Telatar that it can be "understood" by involving an indicator function $I(X;\mathbb{1}_{\{Y=y\}})$, which is a binary random variable. Another one year later, I came arcoss a paper by Peter Grünwald and Paul Vitányi, which explictly define it as $I(X;Y=y)=H(X)-H(X|Y=y)$.

I also received two other points from many people. First, $I(X;Y=y)$ is not well-defined. Second, one can write any math formula they want, as long as it, to some extent, conforms to formal logic. These points are right but it is not the answer I want.

Now, which is another two years later, I am with ChatGPT and writing this blog. I am asking ChatGPT to dig up who is able to dig up lots of papers related to this question, and about the relationship with statistical physics.
